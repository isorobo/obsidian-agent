# Nobel Prize Banquet Speech (2024)

## Citation

Hinton, Geoffrey. "Banquet speech". The Nobel Prize, 10 December 2024. https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/speech/.

## One-line summary

Accepting the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics, Hinton praises neural network progress while warning that profit-driven companies and existing harms put long-term human safety at risk.

## Key claims

- The 2024 Nobel committees in Physics and Chemistry recognised dramatic progress in artificial neural networks.
- Neural networks excel at modelling human intuition rather than human reasoning.
- Intelligent assistants will lift productivity across nearly all industries.
- AI has already produced divisive echo-chambers and enabled surveillance and phishing by authoritarian regimes.
- Digital beings more intelligent than humans pose a long-term existential threat.
- If superintelligent systems are built by companies chasing short-term profits, safety will not be the priority.
- Advanced AI threats are no longer science fiction and require urgent safety research.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Public platform discipline: he uses the Nobel banquet, a ceremonial occasion, to deliver a structured safety warning rather than personal reflection.
- Intuition versus reasoning: he positions neural networks as continuous with human intuition, separating them from the symbolic-reasoning lineage.
- Direct critique of corporate incentives: naming short-term profit as the obstacle to safety departs from the more diplomatic register of his peers.
- Layered risk model: he stacks near-term harms (echo chambers, surveillance, phishing) against long-term existential threat, refusing to treat them as substitutes.
- Refusal of the science-fiction framing: the line "are no longer science fiction" rejects the rhetorical move that dismisses x-risk as speculative.
