# Geoffrey Hinton tells us why he's now scared of the tech he helped build (2023)

## Citation

Heaven, Will Douglas. "Geoffrey Hinton tells us why he's now scared of the tech he helped build". MIT Technology Review, 2 May 2023. https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/05/02/1072528/geoffrey-hinton-google-why-scared-ai/.

## One-line summary

In his first major interview after leaving Google, Hinton describes a sudden shift in his view that large language models may soon exceed human intelligence and explains the risks that follow.

## Key claims

- Hinton has reversed his earlier position on whether digital systems will become more intelligent than humans.
- Large language models behave so differently from humans that he compares them to an alien arrival.
- Confabulation is a feature of human memory, and current models confabulate in the same way.
- Authoritarian actors could weaponise advanced AI to build lethal autonomous systems.
- His departure from Google removes a constraint on speaking openly about safety risks.

## Excerpts

> "I have suddenly switched my views on whether these things are going to be more intelligent than us."
~ MIT Technology Review interview, 2 May 2023

> "These things are totally different from us. Sometimes I think it's as if aliens had landed and people haven't realized because they speak very good English."
~ MIT Technology Review interview, 2 May 2023

> "People always confabulate. Confabulation is a signature of human memory. These models are doing something just like people."
~ MIT Technology Review interview, 2 May 2023

> "I'm mildly depressed. Which is why I'm scared."
~ MIT Technology Review interview, 2 May 2023

> "Don't think for a moment that Putin wouldn't make hyper-intelligent robots with the goal of killing Ukrainians. He wouldn't hesitate."
~ MIT Technology Review interview, 2 May 2023

> "I want to talk about AI safety issues without having to worry about how it interacts with Google's business."
~ MIT Technology Review interview, 2 May 2023

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Revisability: he announces a public reversal of a long-held timeline view and treats updating as a duty rather than a weakness.
- Analogy to alien minds: the language of "aliens landing" signals that he sees neural network cognition as a separate kind of intelligence, not a scaled-up human one.
- Continuity between machine and human error: he reads confabulation in models as evidence of shared mechanism, not of model failure.
- Realist threat assessment: invoking Putin and lethal autonomous systems frames the risk in terms of likely state actors rather than abstract scenarios.
- Institutional independence: he treats leaving Google as the precondition for honest public advocacy.
