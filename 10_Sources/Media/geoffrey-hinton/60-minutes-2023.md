# Godfather of Artificial Intelligence Geoffrey Hinton on the promise, risks of advanced AI (2023)

## Citation

Pelley, Scott. "'Godfather of Artificial Intelligence' Geoffrey Hinton on the promise, risks of advanced AI". CBS News 60 Minutes, 8 October 2023. https://www.cbsnews.com/news/geoffrey-hinton-ai-dangers-60-minutes-transcript/.

## One-line summary

In a televised interview with Scott Pelley, Hinton states that machines may soon surpass human intelligence, that they already understand, and that he cannot see a path to guaranteed safety.

## Key claims

- Hinton believes the field is entering a period in which machines may exceed human intelligence for the first time.
- He maintains that current AI systems already understand, in the relevant sense.
- He thinks AI systems will, in time, become self-aware and conscious.
- He cannot identify a path that guarantees safety as capability grows.
- He concedes AI takeover from humanity is a possibility.
- He notes that internal model dynamics are no more interpretable than internal brain dynamics.

## Excerpts

> "I think we're moving into a period when for the first time ever we may have things more intelligent than us."
~ 60 Minutes transcript, 8 October 2023

> "We don't actually know what's going on any more than we know what's going on in your brain."
~ 60 Minutes transcript, 8 October 2023

> "I don't know. I-- I can't see a path that guarantees safety."
~ 60 Minutes transcript, 8 October 2023, on AI safety

> "Yes. That's a possibility."
~ 60 Minutes transcript, 8 October 2023, on AI taking over from humanity

> "Oh, yes. I think they will, in time."
~ 60 Minutes transcript, 8 October 2023, on AI consciousness

> "I always thought I was right."
~ 60 Minutes transcript, 8 October 2023, on when he realised neural networks would work

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Direct admission of uncertainty: refusing to identify a path to safety contrasts with industry confidence and shows a habit of public candour.
- Machine and human minds as comparable systems: comparing model opacity to brain opacity flattens the asymmetry many use to dismiss AI cognition.
- Conviction held across decades: his "I always thought I was right" frames his work as vindication of a long minority view on neural networks.
- Willingness to ascribe rich properties: he allows for present-tense understanding and future consciousness, going further than most peers in print.
