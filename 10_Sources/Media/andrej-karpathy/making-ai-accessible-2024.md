# Making AI Accessible

## Citation

Karpathy, Andrej, and Stephanie Zhan. "Making AI Accessible". Sequoia AI Ascent 2024, San Francisco, March 2024. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c3b-JASoPi0.

## One-line summary

Karpathy presents the LLM OS vision and argues that AI accessibility depends on balancing open and proprietary models, small technical teams, and building for diverse modalities.

## Key claims

- The LLM OS conceptual model places the transformer as the central processor and treats text, images, and audio as peripherals; the context window is the working memory.
- A significant efficiency gap exists between AI models and the human brain; advances in architecture and precision reduction are necessary to close it.
- The AI ecosystem benefits from balance between proprietary and open-source models, mirroring the operating system landscape.
- Small, highly technical teams without middle management - drawing on Karpathy's Tesla experience - outperform larger, more hierarchical organisations.
- There is "room for an array of applications beyond the default offerings of major players like OpenAI," favouring startup development.
- Reinforcement learning and energy-efficient computing designs offer meaningful near-term improvement paths.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Karpathy extends his LLM OS framing from late 2023 into a product and organisational thesis: the right team structure mirrors the right model architecture - lean, direct, and technical.
- His comparison of AI model competition to the OS landscape (Linux vs. Windows) reflects a consistent preference for pluralism over monopoly in foundational infrastructure.
- His focus on efficiency gaps signals that he sees hardware and architecture constraints as real bottlenecks, not just algorithmic ones.
