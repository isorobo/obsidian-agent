# Senate Judiciary Testimony — Dario Amodei (2023)

## Citation

Amodei, D. (2023). *Written Testimony of Dario Amodei, Ph.D., CEO of Anthropic*. Hearing before the US Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Privacy, Technology, and the Law.

## One-line summary

Formal testimony framing AI progress as national-security critical and arguing for three pillars: supply-chain security, mandatory pre-deployment testing under a NIST-led auditing regime, and research funding for measurement science.

## Key claims

- Progress driven by three compounding factors: chips (2-5x/yr), chip speed (2x per 1-2 years), algorithms (~2x/yr).
- "Extraordinarily grave threats" to US national security within two to three years, especially in biology, cybersecurity, nuclear, and chemistry.
- Relinquishing development in the US hands the technology and its moral authority to non-democratic adversaries.
- Pillar 1: Secure the AI supply chain — semiconductor manufacturing equipment, GPUs, and trained model weights.
- Pillar 2: Mandatory testing-and-auditing regime for powerful new models, analogue to airplane and automobile safety testing.
- Pillar 3: Fund measurement science itself; NIST suggested as lead agency; supports National AI Research Resource.
- Red-teaming research, Constitutional AI, and interpretability cited as Anthropic's safety contributions.
- Pre-deployment testing should cover CBRN, bias, misinformation, privacy, child safety, and autonomy-escape capabilities.

## Excerpts

> "A straightforward extrapolation of the pace of progress suggests that, in 2-3 years, AI systems may facilitate extraordinary insights in broad swaths of many science and engineering disciplines. This will cause a revolution in technology and scientific discovery, but also greatly widen the set of people who can wreak havoc."

> "New AI models should have to pass a rigorous battery of safety tests both during development and before being released to the public or to customers."

> "Our suggestion for the agency to oversee this process is NIST, whose mandate focuses explicitly on measurement and evaluation."
