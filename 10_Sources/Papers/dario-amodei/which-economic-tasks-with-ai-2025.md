# Which Economic Tasks are Performed with AI? (arXiv 2503.04761)

## Citation

Handa, K., et al. (2025). *Which Economic Tasks are Performed with AI? Evidence from Millions of Claude Conversations*. arXiv:2503.04761.

## One-line summary

Privacy-preserving analysis of over four million Claude.ai conversations mapped to the US Department of Labor O*NET occupational framework. Measures AI integration by task depth and augmentation-versus-automation ratio.

## Key claims

- AI usage concentrates in technical and creative sectors. Software development and writing tasks together account for nearly half of all usage.
- Computer and mathematical occupations account for 37.2% of interactions.
- Usage peaks in upper wage quartiles and among occupations requiring bachelor's-degree-level preparation.
- 57% of usage augments human capability; 43% automates.
- 36% of occupations use AI for at least one quarter of their tasks.
- Wholesale automation remains rare: only about 4% of occupations use AI for 75% or more of their tasks.

## Excerpts

> "Our analysis reveals that AI usage primarily concentrates in software development and writing tasks, which together account for nearly half of all total usage. However, usage of AI extends more broadly across the economy, with approximately 36% of occupations using AI for at least a quarter of their associated tasks."

> "57% of usage suggests augmentation of human capabilities ... while 43% suggests automation."

## Critical evaluation

Single-platform data. Self-selected user base biases sectoral distribution. Useful leading indicator rather than population estimate of AI penetration. Complements Brynjolfsson's task-level methodology.
