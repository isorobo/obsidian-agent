# The Forward-Forward Algorithm: Some Preliminary Investigations (2022)

## Citation

Hinton, Geoffrey. "The Forward-Forward Algorithm: Some Preliminary Investigations". arXiv:2212.13345, 2022. https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13345.

## One-line summary

The paper introduces the Forward-Forward algorithm, which replaces backpropagation with two forward passes that contrast real data against negative data and uses a per-layer goodness objective.

## Key claims

- The aim is to introduce a new learning procedure for neural networks and to show that it works on a few small problems.
- The Forward-Forward algorithm replaces the forward and backward passes of backpropagation with two forward passes.
- One forward pass uses positive real data; the other uses negative data, which the network can generate itself.
- Each layer has its own objective: high goodness for positive data, low goodness for negative data.
- The sum of squared activities in a layer can serve as the goodness, with other choices possible.
- Negative passes can be done offline if separated in time, enabling video to be pipelined through without stored activities.

## Excerpts

> "The aim of this paper is to introduce a new learning procedure for neural networks and to demonstrate that it works well enough on a few small problems to be worth further investigation."
~ "Abstract", arXiv:2212.13345

> "The Forward-Forward algorithm replaces the forward and backward passes of backpropagation by two forward passes, one with positive (i.e. real) data and the other with negative data which could be generated by the network itself."
~ "Abstract", arXiv:2212.13345

> "Each layer has its own objective function which is simply to have high goodness for positive data and low goodness for negative data."
~ "Abstract", arXiv:2212.13345

> "The sum of the squared activities in a layer can be used as the goodness but there are many other possibilities, including minus the sum of the squared activities."
~ "Abstract", arXiv:2212.13345

> "If the positive and negative passes could be separated in time, the negative passes could be done offline, which would make the learning much simpler in the positive pass and allow video to be pipelined through the network without ever storing activities or stopping to propagate derivatives."
~ "Abstract", arXiv:2212.13345

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Biological plausibility: by replacing backpropagation with two forward passes, Hinton signals enduring discomfort with the biological realism of the algorithm he popularised.
- Modesty of claim: the title is "Some Preliminary Investigations" and the abstract concedes the algorithm has been tested only on small problems.
- Local objectives: per-layer goodness functions show a preference for distributed learning signals over a global loss gradient.
- Hardware sympathy: the offline negative pass and pipelined video suggest Hinton designs algorithms with attention to physical constraints on neuromorphic and streaming systems.
