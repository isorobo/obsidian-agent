# Machines of Loving Grace

## Citation

Amodei, D. (2024). *Machines of Loving Grace: How AI Could Transform the World for the Better*. darioamodei.com, October 2024.

## One-line summary

Positive-case manifesto for powerful AI: the "country of geniuses in a datacenter" compresses fifty to one hundred years of biomedical and scientific progress into five to ten years, conditional on solving alignment and securing a democratic lead through an entente strategy.

## Key claims

- Defines "powerful AI" (preferred over "AGI") as smarter than a Nobel laureate across most fields, with full virtual interfaces, autonomous multi-week task execution, and millions of parallel instances running at ten to one-hundred times human speed.
- Powerful AI "could come as early as 2026" though longer is possible.
- Rejects both the singularity framing and the saturation framing. Real-world physical and experimental limits bind even a country of geniuses.
- "Compressed 21st century" thesis: AI-accelerated biology and medicine deliver fifty to one-hundred years of progress in five to ten years.
- Near-complete prevention or treatment of natural infectious disease, most cancers, genetic disease, Alzheimer's, and most mental illness within the compressed window.
- "Biological freedom": unprecedented control over weight, appearance, reproduction, and lifespan.
- Developing-world dream scenario: twenty percent annual GDP growth driven by AI finance ministers and technology diffusion, taking sub-Saharan Africa to current-China per-capita GDP in five to ten years.
- Entente strategy: a democratic coalition secures compute supply chain, blocks adversary chip access, and offers AI benefits ("Atoms for Peace" analogue) in exchange for alignment with the coalition.
- Long run: current economic setups stop making sense; universal basic income, AI-capitalist economies, or stranger arrangements required.

## Excerpts

> "I think it could come as early as 2026, though there are also ways it could take much longer."

> "We could summarize this as a 'country of geniuses in a datacenter'. Clearly such an entity would be capable of solving very difficult problems, very fast, but it is not trivial to figure out how fast. Two 'extreme' positions both seem false to me."

> "My basic prediction is that AI-enabled biology and medicine will allow us to compress the progress that human biologists would have achieved over the next 50-100 years into 5-10 years. I'll refer to this as the 'compressed 21st century'."

> "My current guess at the best way to do this is via an 'entente strategy', in which a coalition of democracies seeks to gain a clear advantage (even just a temporary one) on powerful AI by securing its supply chain, scaling quickly, and blocking or delaying adversaries' access to key resources like chips and semiconductor equipment."

## Critical evaluation

Load-bearing optimism about the developing world (twenty percent GDP growth) rests on an unspecified political pathway. The entente strategy assumes adversary compliance once isolated; history of export-control regimes is mixed. The compressed-21st-century thesis is testable on observable milestones (cancer mortality, Alzheimer's biomarkers) within the 2029 to 2034 window.
