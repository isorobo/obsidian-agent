# 2025 LLM Year in Review

## Citation

Karpathy, Andrej. "2025 LLM Year in Review". karpathy.bearblog.dev, December 2025. https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/year-in-review-2025/.

## One-line summary

Karpathy identifies six paradigm shifts in LLMs during 2025 - led by reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards - and argues the field has exploited under 10% of the technology's potential.

## Key claims

- Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) became the dominant training methodology, with models spontaneously developing reasoning-like strategies when trained on verifiable rewards such as maths and code puzzles.
- RLVR consumed compute originally budgeted for pretraining because its capability-per-dollar ratio proved unexpectedly high.
- LLMs should be understood as "summoned ghosts" - digital entities that mimic humans - rather than as growing animals, because their optimisation objective differs fundamentally from biological intelligence.
- "Vibe coding" - building software in English without reading the generated code - marks the crossing of a threshold where programming is no longer reserved for trained professionals.
- Claude Code demonstrated the first convincing LLM agent: extended tool use and reasoning for multi-step problem solving without constant human intervention.
- The field has realised under 10% of the potential of this computing paradigm, even at current capability levels.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- Karpathy's "ghost" metaphor - appearing in both this post and the Dwarkesh interview - marks a deliberate conceptual break with both animal-intelligence and sci-fi-robot framings.
- His treatment of benchmarks as "almost by construction verifiable environments" immediately susceptible to RLVR shows his habit of identifying structural incentive problems in how the field measures progress.
- The 10% potential estimate reveals optimism grounded in structural analysis: the bottleneck is application layer innovation, not model capability, and the upside is therefore large.
