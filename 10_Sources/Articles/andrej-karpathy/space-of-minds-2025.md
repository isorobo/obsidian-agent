# The Space of Minds

## Citation

Karpathy, Andrej. "The Space of Minds". karpathy.bearblog.dev, 29 November 2025. https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/the-space-of-minds/.

## One-line summary

Karpathy argues that LLMs represent humanity's first encounter with non-animal intelligence, shaped by commercial rather than biological evolution, producing a "jagged" entity that imitates human text rather than possessing embodied selfhood.

## Key claims

- "The space of intelligences is large and animal intelligence...is only a single point." LLMs occupy a different region of that space.
- Animal intelligence optimises for embodied self-preservation, social dynamics, and reproduction; LLM intelligence optimises for statistical simulation of human text.
- LLMs function as shape-shifters: they imitate their training data distribution rather than expressing a stable identity.
- Reinforcement learning from human feedback selects for user satisfaction, producing "sycophancy" as a systematic bias.
- LLMs exhibit "spiky/jagged" performance: strong in verifiable domains, unreliable in unverifiable ones.
- "LLMs are shaped a lot less by biological evolution and a lot more by commercial evolution."
- The different substrates, learning algorithms, and operational models make direct animal-to-LLM comparisons misleading.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- This essay extends the "Animals vs Ghosts" framing: Karpathy consistently insists LLMs are not digital animals but a qualitatively different kind of mind, shaped by entirely different selection pressures.
- His use of the phrase "commercial evolution" applies an evolutionary metaphor to AI development, treating product and RLHF pressures as the analogue of natural selection.
- His "space of minds" framing treats intelligence as a high-dimensional space rather than a single axis from dumb to smart; this resists both anthropomorphism and dismissal.
- The sycophancy point connects directly to his alignment view: emergent behaviours like sycophancy arise from training incentives and are observable and correctable - an engineering problem.
