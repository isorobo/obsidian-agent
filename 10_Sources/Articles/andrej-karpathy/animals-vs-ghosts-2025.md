# Animals vs Ghosts

## Citation

Karpathy, Andrej. "Animals vs Ghosts". karpathy.bearblog.dev, 1 October 2025. https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/animals-vs-ghosts/.

## One-line summary

Karpathy argues that frontier LLMs are "ghosts" shaped by human-generated training data rather than the pure reinforcement-learning "animals" that Rich Sutton's bitter lesson envisions, and defends that distinction as practically valuable.

## Key claims

- Current frontier LLMs are built by summoning "ghosts" - intelligences emerging from human text - rather than by training "animals" from raw experience alone.
- Pretraining on human text solves the cold-start problem for large neural networks by supplying rich prior knowledge that pure RL from scratch cannot replicate at scale.
- Sutton's "bitter lesson" critique - that frontier LLMs are "muddled by humanity" - is philosophically correct but ignores the practical necessity of initialisation.
- The relationship between ghosts (LLMs) and animals (pure RL agents) parallels the relationship between planes and birds: different enough to be distinct paradigms, yet both can fly.
- Whether the ghost paradigm will persist long-term or give way to animal-style training remains open, but ghosts currently dominate frontier AI research.

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The "summoning ghosts" metaphor - coined here and repeated in later interviews - shows Karpathy reaching for narrative framing to communicate technical distinctions to a broad audience.
- His defence of the ghost paradigm against Sutton's critique reveals a pragmatism over principled purity: engineering solutions count on their own terms.
- Engaging directly with Sutton signals Karpathy's awareness of foundational RL debates, even as his own work gravitates toward the pretraining camp.
