# Verifiability

## Citation

Karpathy, Andrej. "Verifiability". karpathy.bearblog.dev, 17 November 2025. https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/verifiability/.

## One-line summary

Karpathy proposes "verifiability" as the key predictor of which tasks AI will automate next, replacing the earlier Software 1.0 criterion of "specifiability."

## Key claims

- In the Software 1.0 era, a task was automatable if it could be specified as explicit rules; specifiability was the bottleneck.
- In the Software 2.0 era, the bottleneck shifts to verifiability: a task is automatable if its outcome can be checked reliably by a reward signal.
- A task is verifiable when its environment is resettable, the evaluation is efficient, and a scalar reward distinguishes better from worse outcomes.
- Tasks with high verifiability - mathematics, coding, games with clear win conditions - are advancing rapidly under AI.
- Tasks with low verifiability - creative strategy, interpersonal judgement, open-ended research - lag behind because feedback loops are weak or absent.
- The Software 2.0 automation wave will therefore hit verifiable domains first and hardest, regardless of whether those domains seem "technical."

## Excerpts

> "Software 1.0 easily automates what you can specify. Software 2.0 easily automates what you can verify."
> ~ Core argument section

## Reveals about tendency of thought

- The specifiability-to-verifiability progression extends the Software 2.0 framework Karpathy introduced in 2017, showing that he updates his own frameworks systematically rather than abandoning them.
- The focus on reward signals and resettable environments reflects his RL background and his time supervising Autopilot, where closed-loop simulation provided exactly such verifiable feedback.
- Treating verifiability as the automation frontier provides a practical tool for predicting AI impact without relying on AGI framing, consistent with his stated preference for engineering over philosophy.
