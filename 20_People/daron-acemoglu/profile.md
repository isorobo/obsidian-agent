# Daron Acemoglu

## One-line thesis

AI macro-productivity estimates are overstated; the technology's labour-market impact depends on the direction of innovation, and current incentives skew toward "so-so" automation that displaces workers without raising productivity.

## Current role and affiliations

Institute Professor at MIT. 2024 Nobel laureate in Economic Sciences (jointly with Johnson and Robinson) for institutional economics.

## Stated positions

### On AGI timelines

Sceptical of lab-insider timelines; treats transformational claims as marketing-inflected.

### On economic impact

Modest aggregate productivity uplift; large distributional consequences.

### On policy

Redirect AI research toward human-complementary tools. Tax automation. Labour institutions matter more than the technology itself.

## Intellectual trajectory

Institutional economics with James Robinson: Why Nations Fail (2012). Power and Progress with Simon Johnson (2023). "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI" (2024).
